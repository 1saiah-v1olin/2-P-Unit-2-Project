Pines of Rome

By Isaiah Brown

Pines of Rome is a symphonic tone poem created by Italian composer Ottorino Respighi in 1924 as a part of the Roman Trilogy. The piece starts in the famous Villa Borghese, depicting children running around, playing, and having fun.

The second movement starts without pause from the 1st movement, immediately transporting the listener into the Roman Catacombs. Unlike the Catacombs in Pictures at an Exhibition, instead of being dark and unstable with heavy brass chords, Respighi depicts these Catacombs as an atmospheric, almost otherworldy experience.

The 3rd movement also starts without pause, taking the listener from the world of the Roman catacombs to the Janiculum hill, depicting the grandeur and beauty of Rome at night.


The 4th movement brings us to the Appian way, one of the earliest and most important Roman roads, depicting the return of the Roman legions to the city.
